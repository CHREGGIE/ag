## What This Skill Does

Generates legally compliant marketing content for New York business law and contracts attorneys. Every output enforces 8 compliance moats that general AI tools don't apply — protecting attorneys from grievance committee complaints, FTC actions, Part 137 fee arbitrations, and Part 1215 engagement-letter violations while producing marketing that converts business clients ethically.

**4 prompt modules:**
1. **Client Acquisition Campaigns** — Google Ads, LinkedIn, email intake sequences
2. **Digital Advertising Suite** — LSA, RSA ad groups, GBP posts, LinkedIn thought leadership
3. **Website Content & Schema** — practice area pages, attorney bio, FAQ, JSON-LD
4. **Reputation & Referral Program** — review sequences, CPA/banker/VC referral network, LinkedIn authority content

### Required inputs (ask if missing)
- Firm name, entity type (PLLC / LLP / PC / sole practitioner), principal office address + phone (Rule 7.1(h))
- Responsible attorney name(s); "Admitted in New York" (+ other jurisdictions); OCA attorney registration no. optional
- Office locations / counties served (NYC boroughs, Long Island, Westchester, upstate)
- Services + fee structure (hourly, flat, subscription/general-counsel retainer, equity)
- Any certifications from ABA-accredited organizations (Rule 7.4)
- Any past results or client testimonials proposed for use (with client written consent status)

## The 8 Compliance Moats

### Moat 1: Rule 7.1 Advertising Labels, Results & Testimonials [ANCHOR]
**What every AI tool generates:** "We'll protect your business." "Win your contract dispute." "We guarantee results." "New York's top business attorney." "Manhattan's best startup lawyer." Fictional client stories with no disclosure.

**What New York requires (22 NYCRR 1200.0, Rule 7.1):**
- **Rule 7.1(f) — "Attorney Advertising" label.** Every advertisement (other than radio, TV, billboard, directory, newspaper/magazine/periodical ads and related websites, or permitted in-person communications) must be labeled "Attorney Advertising" on the first page — or the **home page** of a website. Self-mailers/postcards carry it on the piece. **Email: the subject line must contain "ATTORNEY ADVERTISING."**
- **Rule 7.1(h) — Identification.** Every advertisement must include the name, principal law office address, and telephone number of the lawyer or firm.
- **Rule 7.1(a)/(c) — No false, deceptive, or misleading content.** No guarantees. No nickname, moniker, motto, or trade name implying an ability to obtain results ("The Deal Closer," "Contract Crusher"). No actors portraying lawyers/clients or dramatized scenes without disclosure. No paid endorsements without disclosure.
- **Rule 7.1(d)/(e) — Results, comparisons, testimonials, quality claims.** Statements likely to create expectations about results, comparisons with other lawyers, client testimonials/endorsements, and statements characterizing the quality of services are permitted **only** if factually supported as of the date published **and** accompanied by the disclaimer: **"Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome."** Testimonials require the client's prior written consent and may not concern a matter still pending.
- **Rule 7.1(k) — Retention.** Keep a copy of every advertisement for 3 years (1 year for computer-accessed communications). Preserve website content at launch, at each major redesign or meaningful content change, and no less than every 90 days.
- **FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, 2023 rev.) + Consumer Review Rule (16 CFR Part 465):** "We saved a client $2M in a contract dispute" implies typicality — disclose atypicality in the same visual field.

**What this skill outputs:** "Attorney Advertising" on every home page, first page, mailer, and email subject line. Name/address/phone block on every ad. "Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome." adjacent to any result, testimonial, rating, or comparison. Process-, thoroughness-, and risk-reduction-focused benefit language. No guarantees, no results-implying slogans, no undisclosed dramatizations. A retention reminder with every output.

### Moat 2: Rule 7.4 / 7.5 Specialization, Superlatives & Domain Names
**What every AI tool generates:** "Contract law specialist." "Board-certified business attorney." "Specializing in M&A." "NYC's #1 corporate firm." Domain names like `winyourbusinesscase.com`.

**What New York requires:**
- **Rule 7.4(a):** A lawyer may state they practice in, or limit practice to, particular areas — but may **not** state they are a "specialist" or "specialize" in a field **unless** certified by an organization accredited by the ABA (or as otherwise permitted for patent/admiralty). New York has **no state-run certification** for business law or contracts.
- **Rule 7.4(c):** If an ABA-accredited private certification is claimed, the ad must name the certifying body and state: *"The [name of private certifying organization] is not affiliated with any governmental authority. Certification is not a requirement for the practice of law in the State of New York and does not necessarily indicate greater competence than other attorneys experienced in this field of law."*
- **Superlatives and ratings:** "Best," "#1," "top" are quality/comparison claims under 7.1(d) — permitted only if factually supportable and carrying the past-results disclaimer. Third-party ratings (Super Lawyers, Best Lawyers, Martindale) must accurately name the rating source, year, and not imply it was awarded by a court or bar.
- **Rule 7.5(e):** A domain name, social handle, or trade name may not imply an ability to obtain results and must not be misleading. If the domain isn't the firm name, the firm name must also appear on the site.
- **NYSBA Social Media Ethics Guidelines:** LinkedIn "Skills," "Specialties," and endorsement features can create 7.4 problems — use "Practice areas" / "Focus areas" and turn off or curate "specialist" endorsements.

**What this skill outputs:** "Focuses on," "concentrates in," "practice is limited to" — never "specialist/specializes" unless an ABA-accredited certification is supplied (then the full 7.4(c) disclaimer is auto-inserted). Superlatives removed unless substantiated + disclaimed. Domain/handle suggestions screened for results implications.

### Moat 3: Fee Transparency Gate — Rule 1.5 + 22 NYCRR Part 1215 + Part 137
**What every AI tool generates:** "$499 all-in LLC formation." "Flat-fee contract review." "Affordable business attorney." In New York these are especially misleading because of **LLC publication costs** (Moat 4) and NYC taxes.

**What New York requires:**
- **Rule 1.5(a):** No excessive fees. **Rule 1.5(b):** Communicate scope of representation and basis of fee/expenses before or within a reasonable time after commencing representation.
- **22 NYCRR Part 1215:** A **written letter of engagement (or signed retainer agreement)** is required before or within a reasonable time after starting work, explaining scope, fees, expenses, and billing practices — unless the fee is expected to be **less than $3,000**, the services are of the same general kind previously rendered to and paid for by the client, or another exception applies.
- **22 NYCRR Part 137:** The engagement letter must advise the client of the right to arbitrate fee disputes under the Fee Dispute Resolution Program (generally disputes $1,000–$50,000).
- **Common NY flat-fee traps:**
  - "LLC formation $X" without disclosing the **$200 DOS filing fee**, **LLC Law § 206 publication costs** (can exceed $1,000 in New York County), $50 Certificate of Publication filing, registered agent fees (if any), and the annual **IT-204-LL filing fee** for partnership-taxed LLCs (⚠️ VERIFY schedule).
  - "Contract review $Y" without scope (page count, clause types, number of revision rounds, turnaround).
  - "Startup package" that excludes the operating agreement / shareholders' agreement — the document NY's LLC Law defaults make most important (absent an operating agreement, default LLC Law rules govern).
  - "General counsel subscription" without stating what's excluded (litigation, regulatory filings, out-of-pocket costs) and how overages are billed.

**What this skill outputs:** Every price-forward ad carries scope language and "plus government filing and publication fees" (or equivalent). "All-in" claims trigger an included/excluded list. No "affordable/cheap" without fee context. Every intake sequence includes a Part 1215 engagement-letter step (with Part 137 arbitration notice) before work begins.

### Moat 4: New York Entity Formation Accuracy Gate (LLC Law / BCL / Tax)
**Why New York formation marketing is uniquely error-prone:** Templates written for Delaware, Wyoming, or Nevada get recycled — and New York is not a "no-tax, low-maintenance" state.

**The accuracy failures:**

**New York LLC (Limited Liability Company Law):**
- **LLC Law § 206 publication requirement:** Within **120 days** of filing Articles of Organization, the LLC must publish a notice in **two newspapers** (one weekly, one daily, designated by the county clerk of the county of the office) once a week for **six consecutive weeks**, then file a **Certificate of Publication** with DOS. Failure **suspends the LLC's authority to carry on business** until cured. Cost varies dramatically by county (New York County is typically the most expensive). Any "form your NY LLC for $X" ad that omits publication is materially misleading.
- **Biennial Statement:** Due every two years with DOS ($9 fee).
- **NY LLC Transparency Act (LLC Law § 1106-1107):** Requires LLCs formed or authorized to do business in NY to file beneficial-ownership information (or an exemption attestation) with DOS. **⚠️ VERIFY** current effective dates, the treatment of domestic vs. foreign LLCs after FinCEN's 2025 CTA interim final rule, and any 2025–2026 amendments before referencing deadlines or penalties.
- **PLLCs (LLC Law Art. 12):** Licensed professionals (CPAs, architects, physicians, attorneys) must form PLLCs with licensing-authority certification — a common omission in "any business can form an LLC" content.

**New York Corporation (Business Corporation Law):**
- DOS filing fee + organization tax on authorized shares; Biennial Statement.
- **New York has corporate franchise tax (Tax Law Art. 9-A), personal income tax, and — inside the five boroughs — NYC Business Corporation Tax / General Corporation Tax and the Unincorporated Business Tax (UBT) on partnership-taxed LLCs and sole proprietors.** Never market NY formation as "tax-free." **⚠️ VERIFY** current rates and MCTMT (Metropolitan Commuter Transportation Mobility Tax) thresholds for MCTD counties.
- **"Delaware vs. New York":** Delaware remains common for VC-backed startups (Court of Chancery, investor familiarity) — but a Delaware entity operating in NY must still register as a foreign entity in NY (and a foreign LLC must still satisfy § 802 publication). Never imply forming out of state avoids NY obligations.

**What this skill outputs:** Formation marketing that accurately describes NY's real advantages (NYC access to capital and talent, sophisticated Commercial Division of the Supreme Court for business disputes, flexible LLC Law) while disclosing: publication requirement and cost range, biennial statements, LLC Transparency Act status, NY/NYC tax exposure, and the foreign-registration requirement for Delaware entities.

### Moat 5: New York Restrictive Covenant & Employment-Contract Gate
**Why this is the most dangerous drafting-marketing failure in NY business law:** Non-compete law in New York is unsettled — repeated legislative attempts, a vetoed statewide ban, a vacated FTC rule — and most marketing copy is stale.

**The current framework:**
- **No general statute (as of this writing — ⚠️ VERIFY).** Governor Hochul **vetoed** the 2023 statewide ban (S3100A) in December 2023. Successor bills (e.g., 2025's S4641, with an earnings threshold) have been introduced; check status before referencing. NYC Council has also introduced ban proposals.
- **Common-law reasonableness — *BDO Seidman v. Hirshberg*, 93 N.Y.2d 382 (1999):** A covenant is enforceable only if it (1) is no greater than required to protect a **legitimate interest** (trade secrets, confidential information, client goodwill, or **unique/extraordinary** services), (2) does not impose undue hardship on the employee, and (3) does not injure the public. Courts may partially enforce (blue-pencil) only absent overreaching.
- **Involuntary termination:** Under *Post v. Merrill Lynch*, 48 N.Y.2d 84 (1979), and progeny, covenants may be unenforceable against employees terminated **without cause** (the "employee choice" doctrine); appellate departments vary.
- **Labor Law § 202-k:** Non-competes are void for broadcast employees.
- **FTC Non-Compete Rule:** Set aside nationwide (*Ryan LLC v. FTC*, N.D. Tex. Aug. 2024); FTC abandoned its appeal in 2025. Do not reference it as in force; FTC case-by-case enforcement remains possible.
- **Related contract rules marketers miss:**
  - **General Obligations Law § 5-336:** Limits nondisclosure/confidentiality terms in settlements of discrimination, harassment, and retaliation claims (preference of complainant, consideration period, no liquidated damages/forfeiture for breach, as amended 2023).
  - **Labor Law § 740:** Whistleblower protections — NDAs cannot be marketed as silencing protected reporting; employers must post notice.
  - **Freelance Isn't Free Act** (statewide — General Business Law Art. 44-A, effective Aug. 28, 2024; NYC Admin. Code § 20-927 et seq. since 2017): written contracts required for freelance engagements of **$800+** (alone or aggregated over 120 days), with payment-timing and anti-retaliation rules.

**What this skill outputs:** No "ironclad," "airtight," or "bulletproof" non-compete language. "We draft restrictive covenants tailored to protect legitimate business interests under New York law" replaces blanket promises. Pending-legislation caveat on any non-compete content. Garden-leave, non-solicit, and confidentiality alternatives positioned accurately. Freelance contract offers reference FIFA requirements.

### Moat 6: Rule 1.8(a) Equity-for-Fees (Business Transactions with Clients) Gate
**The fastest-growing ethics risk in NYC startup law:** Silicon Alley firms increasingly take equity (stock, SAFEs, options) in lieu of or alongside fees.

**What New York Rule 1.8(a) requires:**
- The transaction and terms are **fair and reasonable** to the client and **fully disclosed in writing** in a manner the client can reasonably understand.
- The client is **advised in writing** of the desirability of seeking, and given a reasonable opportunity to seek, **independent counsel**.
- The client gives **informed consent, in a writing signed by the client**, to the essential terms and the lawyer's role (including whether the lawyer represents the client in the transaction).
- Also consider **Rule 1.5(a)** (reasonableness of the total fee including equity value), **Rule 1.7** (personal-interest conflicts as the lawyer's financial stake grows), and **Rule 1.8(i)** limits on acquiring a proprietary interest in the subject of litigation.

**Marketing exposure:** "We invest in our clients" / "we bet on founders" frames a conflict as a favor. Advertising equity arrangements without the 1.8(a) process misrepresents them as a simple alternative to fees and becomes Exhibit A in a later malpractice or grievance matter.

**What this skill outputs:** Any equity reference includes accurate disclosure language ("Equity-based fee arrangements are available in limited circumstances and require written disclosure, the opportunity to consult independent counsel, and your signed informed consent"). "Partner with founders" framing replaced with fee-structure transparency. Startup intake sequences include an equity-arrangement disclosure step where applicable.

### Moat 7: UPL, Online Legal Services & Solicitation Gate (Judiciary Law + Rule 7.3)
**The positioning battle:** LegalZoom, Rocket Lawyer, ZenBusiness, Northwest, and "publication services" compete for NY LLC formation, contract templates, and compliance.

**The compliance exposure:**
- **Judiciary Law §§ 478, 484, 485, 495:** Practicing law without admission is a misdemeanor (with felony exposure under § 485-a in aggravated cases); corporations may not practice law. "We do what LegalZoom does, but better" without explaining *what* a lawyer does differently misses the value proposition.
- **False comparisons (Rule 7.1(d)):** Comparing your services with other providers requires factual support and the past-results disclaimer.
- **Don't compete on speed** — compete on what licensed attorneys provide: legal advice, interpretation, negotiation, dispute counseling, ongoing compliance, attorney-client privilege.
- **Rule 7.3 solicitation:** Targeted outreach (cold email, LinkedIn InMail, direct mail to a predetermined recipient whose primary purpose is retention) is a **solicitation**. It may not be made in person or by telephone/real-time interactive communication (except to close friends, relatives, former or existing clients); written solicitations must be **filed with the attorney disciplinary committee** of the judicial district where the lawyer maintains a principal office (Rule 7.3(c)) and carry the "Attorney Advertising" label. Retain copies.
- **Rule 7.2(a) — No paying for referrals:** No payment or anything of value to a person or organization to recommend or obtain employment (limited exceptions, e.g., the reasonable cost of advertising, qualified lawyer-referral services). Reciprocal referral relationships with nonlawyer professionals must comply with **Rule 5.8** and must not be exclusive or undisclosed.

**What this skill outputs:** Competitive positioning based on attorney-only value. UPL boundary accurately drawn. Every cold-outreach template flagged "SOLICITATION — file under Rule 7.3(c) before sending" with the "ATTORNEY ADVERTISING" subject line. Referral-program content with no compensation for referrals.

### Moat 8: Securities / Capital-Raising Gate (SEC Reg D / Reg CF + Martin Act)
**Why this matters:** NYC is the second-largest venture market in the U.S.; "we'll help you raise your seed round" creates expectations governed by federal and New York securities law.

**The framework:**
- **Securities Act § 4(a)(2) / Regulation D:** Rule 506(b) — no general solicitation, unlimited accredited + up to 35 sophisticated non-accredited investors. Rule 506(c) — general solicitation permitted, accredited investors only, reasonable steps to verify (SEC staff's March 2025 no-action guidance on high minimum-investment amounts as a verification method). **Form D** due within 15 days of first sale.
- **Regulation Crowdfunding (Title III JOBS Act):** Up to $5M per 12 months via a registered portal; Form C.
- **New York notice filing:** Rule 506 offerings require a New York notice filing with the Attorney General's Investor Protection Bureau (via NASAA EFD) and fee (⚠️ VERIFY current timing and fee). Broker-dealer/finder issues under GBL § 359-e.
- **Martin Act (GBL Art. 23-A):** The NY Attorney General has broad anti-fraud authority without proof of scienter — overstating returns or "guaranteed" raises in founder-facing marketing creates real exposure.
- **Lawyers don't raise capital.** Marketing implying the firm will "get you funded" or "introduce investors for a fee" risks unregistered broker-dealer issues and Rule 7.1 violations.

**What this skill outputs:** Capital-raising copy references Reg D (506(b) vs. 506(c)), Reg CF, Form D, and NY notice filing accurately. "We'll help you raise money" → "We provide securities-law counsel for private placements, SAFEs, convertible notes, and Reg CF offerings." No guaranteed-raise language. No investor-introduction-for-fee offers.

## Prompt Modules

Each module runs every draft through the Pre-Publish Gate (below) before output, and returns: (1) the content, (2) a **Compliance Log** listing every moat triggered and the fix applied, (3) any **⚠️ VERIFY** items the attorney must confirm.

### Module 1 — Client Acquisition Campaigns
- **Google Ads search campaigns** by practice area (formation, contracts, M&A, disputes/Commercial Division, startups/venture, employment agreements). Headlines/descriptions free of guarantees, "specialist," and results-implying superlatives; firm name + phone in ad assets or landing page per 7.1(h).
- **LinkedIn campaigns** (Sponsored Content, Thought Leader Ads). InMail/Message Ads = solicitation → 7.3(c) filing flag.
- **Email intake sequence** (5–7 emails): every subject line contains "ATTORNEY ADVERTISING"; includes conflict-check step, Part 1215 engagement letter + Part 137 notice step, no advice before engagement, unsubscribe (CAN-SPAM).

### Module 2 — Digital Advertising Suite
- **Google Local Services Ads** profile copy (verify LSA "Google Screened" badge wording isn't presented as a bar credential).
- **RSA ad groups** (15 headlines / 4 descriptions per group) with negative-keyword suggestions to filter out litigation-only or employee-side queries if not served.
- **Google Business Profile posts** (weekly cadence) — educational, with firm identification.
- **LinkedIn thought-leadership posts** — NY-law updates (LLC Transparency Act, non-compete bills, FIFA, Commercial Division rules) with "this is general information, not legal advice" framing; posts referencing results get the disclaimer.

### Module 3 — Website Content & Schema
- **Home page** with "Attorney Advertising" label and identification block.
- **Practice area pages** (formation & governance, commercial contracts, M&A, venture/startups, restrictive covenants, commercial disputes) — Moats 3–5 and 8 applied.
- **Attorney bios:** admissions (court, year), education, focus areas; "specialist" only per 7.4(c); bar association memberships described accurately (NYSBA Business Law Section, NYC Bar committees).
- **FAQ pages** written for AI-search/featured snippets ("Do I have to publish my New York LLC?", "Is a non-compete enforceable in New York?").
- **JSON-LD:** `LegalService` + `Attorney` (Person) + `FAQPage` + `BreadcrumbList`; `areaServed` by county/borough; no `aggregateRating` from self-hosted reviews (Google policy) and no rating markup implying results.

### Module 4 — Reputation & Referral Program
- **Review request sequence** (post-matter): ask all clients (no review gating), no incentives conditioned on sentiment (FTC 16 CFR Part 465), no ghost-written reviews, respond to reviews without revealing confidential information (**Rule 1.6** — never confirm client status or matter details in public replies).
- **CPA / banker / wealth-manager / VC referral network:** educational co-hosted events and content; **no referral fees or anything of value for referrals (Rule 7.2(a))**; Rule 5.8 compliance for any formal nonlawyer-professional arrangement.
- **LinkedIn authority content** calendar (12 weeks) — testimonial or results posts carry consent + disclaimer requirements.
